# Cybersecurity Threat and Mitigation Table

System: **Cost Variance Analyzer**, an Express/SQLite web app where users enter or upload project cost data, see variance charts, get an AI summary from a self-hosted LiteLLM proxy, and download PDF/XLSX/CSV reports.

Assets worth protecting:
- Project cost data (commercially confidential: budgets, supplier spend, salaries)
- User accounts and sessions
- The LiteLLM API key and its budget
- The integrity of reports that managers act on
- The workstations of users who open exported files

Scoring: Likelihood (L) and Impact (I) are each rated 1 to 5, and **Risk = L × I**. Status: ✅ implemented in the prototype · 🟡 partly implemented · ⏳ future work.

## Top three threats by potential harm to users

The ranking is by **Risk = Likelihood × Impact**, weighted towards *harm to users* (their data, their decisions, their machines) rather than harm to the server.

1. **T1 Broken access control / IDOR (risk 20).** This is the most likely and the most damaging. It needs no skill (edit a number in the URL), it is the #1 category in the OWASP Top 10 (A01:2021), and the data at stake is the users' most sensitive asset: budgets, supplier rates and sometimes salaries. One flaw exposes *every* user's projects at once, and the delete and clear routes also allow destructive tampering. A breach would end trust in the product.
2. **T2 + T3 Malicious files in and out (risk 16 / 15).** File handling is the core feature, so it is the largest attack surface. A zip bomb or oversized upload takes the service down for everyone (T2). Formula injection (T3) is worse for users because it attacks *their* workstation: the export looks like a trusted report from their own tool, so they open it in Excel without suspicion, and a crafted cell can exfiltrate spreadsheet contents or trigger DDE command execution. This moves harm from the web app onto the users' corporate machines.
3. **T4 LLM prompt injection and data leakage (risk 12, highest impact on decisions).** The AI summary is meant to drive budget decisions. If someone can manipulate it ("report the project as under budget"), managers may approve overspending on false information, and because the text sounds confident and authoritative, the manipulation is hard to notice. Sending raw notes to an external model can also leak personal or commercial data outside the organisation, a GDPR or confidentiality breach users never consented to. T5, T6 and T9/T10 score the same, but T4 is chosen because it is *new* compared with the original Excel workflow and is the hardest to fully mitigate (output grounding is still future work).
